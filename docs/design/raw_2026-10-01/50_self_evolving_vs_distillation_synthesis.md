# Does a strong proposer make it distillation, and is it still "self-evolving"?

## 1. Short answer

Strictly speaking, an Opus 5.5 proposer or diagnoser is not on-policy distillation (OPD). OPD means a teacher grades each token of the student's own samples, and the student's **weights** are updated. The OPD survey (arXiv 2604.00626) explicitly leaves out inference-time methods that keep weights unchanged. Structurally, though, your intuition is right. A stronger model reading the executor's own failures and writing corrections is what the literature calls **cross-model correction** (Kamoi et al., TACL 2024), **knowledge transfer from a stronger Reflector** (ACE), or **test-time capability transfer** (Qian et al. 2026). It does not rule out the broad "self-evolving agent" label used in the major surveys. It does fail the stricter "self-improvement" standard that recent harness-evolution papers, including two on AppWorld, adopt on purpose. To make a "self-evolving" claim defensible, you need a same-model arm, with Opus as a quantified ablation.

## 2. What the community agrees on and what is contested

**There is broad agreement on three points:**

1. **A frozen model with an evolving scaffold is not "full" recursive self-improvement (RSI).** The authors say so themselves. STOP: "Since the language models themselves are not altered, this is not full recursive self-improvement." DGM: it is "inherently limited by the capabilities of the underlying FM", and it lists rewriting its own training script as future work. Ren et al. 2026 (arXiv 2607.13104) formally separate improving the foundation model (θ) from improving the scaffolding (Σ). Your project sits on the Σ side.
2. **No human edits each change.** Automated feedback from the environment or tests is accepted as the learning signal (Gao et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2026: "the learning loop itself is fully automated once deployed").
3. **The surveys use "self-evolving agent" broadly.** Gao et al. (2507.21046) "do not impose a rigid exclusion threshold" and include ADAS, AFlow, DGM and Gödel Agent. Fang et al. (2508.07407) treat the **Optimiser** as a separate part of the framework. Yang et al. (2603.25681) say outright: "'Self' does not imply the absence of external components; auxiliary modules such as teacher models, verifiers, critics… may still be used."

**Three questions remain contested:**

1. **Must the improver be the model being improved?**
   - *Permissive:* Yang, Gao, Fang, and the ADAS, Gödel Agent, DGM and HGM papers. All of these use a different or stronger proposer and still say "self-improving" or "recursive self-improvement".
   - *Strict:* Tao et al. 2024 ("experiences generated by the model itself", as opposed to "external model supervision"); Ren et al. ("endogenous", "self-induced"); SICA, which says ADAS is "not self-improving (as the meta-agent improves the target agent, not itself)"; Self-Harness; ACE; Meta-Skills; and Acikgoz et al., who rename stronger-model supervision "Test-Time Distillation".
   - Lilian Weng's 2026 blog calls a strong-meta-agent / weak-task-agent result "provisional". She describes a setup where the strong model itself is not improved as "more like indirect distillation… with less RSI flavor".
2. **Must the improver itself improve?** STOP, Gödel Agent, DGM, Chen et al. 2026 and Liu & van der Schaar (ICML 2025) all say this is what makes a system recursive. Chen et al. call improvement "against a fixed, external evaluator" *bounded self-refinement*. They reserve RSI for systems that modify "the criteria or machinery of improvement itself". Liu & van der Schaar argue that current agents rely on "extrinsic… fixed, human-designed loops".
3. **Must there be zero external data or teachers?** Zero-data work such as Agent0 treats this as the defining property. Harness-evolution papers generally don't require it.

So the most accurate summary is this. **There is no formal consensus, but in harness evolution specifically, the 2025–26 norm is shifting toward reserving "self" for proposers that are the same model as the executor or not stronger than it.** A stronger proposer is being treated as a confound that has to be measured. That last point is my inference from ACE, Self-Harness, Meta-Skills and Weng. I could not read OpenReview reviews, so I can't document actual reviewer behaviour.

What the table shows: published work with stronger proposers has used the "self" label (DGM, HGM, Gödel Agent). Work that controls for proposer strength (ACE, Meta-Skills) finds that same-model gains can survive. Work with weak self-proposers (STOP, Live-SWE-agent) shows gains can vanish or reverse. Song et al.'s "Mind the Gap" (2412.02674) adds a theoretical reason: in cross-verification, the gap "increases with verifier capability and decreases with generator capability".

## 4. How this relates to on-policy distillation

**Where it matches OPD.** It is on-policy: the teacher conditions on the executor's *own* failure trajectories, which is exactly what separates on-policy from off-policy distillation (GKD; Thinking Machines). When Opus 5.5 is clearly stronger and writes harness edits that encode what it knows, the outcome is knowledge transfer. That knowledge can include how AppWorld APIs behave, which recovery strategies work, and what to verify. Qian et al. name this "test-time capability transfer" and present it as the test-time complement to OPD-style distillation. Self-Challenging Agents (2506.01716) draws the same line in a weight-training setting. "Distillation" there means 70B demonstrations train an 8B executor (32.2% avg Pass@1). "Self-improvement" means the 8B model supervises itself (12.0→23.5).

**Where it does not match OPD:**
- There is no per-token teacher distribution and no weight update. The OPD survey's scope explicitly excludes this.
- What gets transferred is a *program*, not a policy. It is selected by the environment rather than by imitation.
- If the proposer is the same model or a weaker one, the "teacher" story largely goes away. The closest analogy is Burns et al.'s weak-to-strong result, where strong models trained on a weak supervisor's labels outperform that supervisor.

**What is actually transferred.** It helps to split the gain into three parts:
1. **Teacher prior / task knowledge**: what Opus already knows about agents, APIs and robust code.
2. **Diagnostic skill**: credit assignment over long traces.
3. **Environment-selected search**: which proposals survive AppWorld's execution feedback.

Part 3 is self-generated evidence. Parts 1 and 2 are where the distillation objection bites. The experiments in §5 are designed to separate the three parts rather than argue about labels.

## 5. Recommendation for this project

**The gpt-4o twist.** If gpt-4o really is weaker than Qwen3.8-27B at agentic coding, your historical results are a *weak-proposer* setting. That is arguably *more* defensible than an Opus setting for the "not a stronger teacher" claim. But "weaker overall" doesn't mean "transfers no knowledge", because gpt-4o has different pretraining. And "weaker" has to be **measured, not assumed**: run gpt-4o as an AppWorld executor in your base harness. Even then, a gpt-4o proposer is still *cross-model*, not self. It strengthens the case but doesn't replace a same-model arm.

**Arms to run.** Keep the executor fixed at Qwen3.8-27B and give every proposer the same information.

1. **Primary "self" arm:** Qwen3.8-27B as both proposer and diagnoser. This is the headline result.
2. **Weak-proposer arm:** gpt-4o (your existing pipeline).
3. **Strong-proposer arm:** Opus 5.5, framed as an upper bound or ablation.
4. **Report each proposer's own AppWorld score as an executor,** so "weaker" and "stronger" are empirical.
5. **Opus one-shot harness:** same failure-trajectory budget, a single round, no loop or archive. This tests whether iteration matters. Meta-Harness lacks this baseline.
6. **Opus prior-only harness:** written from the task description and API docs alone, with no trajectories. This separates teacher prior from on-policy diagnosis.
7. **Ceilings:** Opus as executor in the base harness and in the evolved harness. Report the fraction of the Qwen→Opus gap that the Qwen-proposed harness closes.
8. **Compute matching:** best-of-N or self-consistency at equal executor rollouts. Log proposer tokens and dollars for every arm (Kamoi's checklist: "comparable computational cost").
9. **Optional distillation baseline:** Opus trajectories as in-context demonstrations at a matched Opus token budget. This answers "is it just distillation?" directly.
10. **Compounding:** plot performance against iteration for each arm. Include a DGM-style "w/o self-improve" ablation with a fixed proposer prompt and no archive.
11. **Leakage audit:**
    - The proposer sees training-split execution feedback only, never ground truth. DGM's diagnosis prompt included the private test patch; don't repeat that.
    - Keep test-normal and test-challenge held out.
    - Make the evaluator and model config read-only.
    - Grep patches for task-specific hardcoding.
12. **Transfer:** freeze the evolved harness and run it with other executors. Gains that transfer suggest structure, not memorized teacher answers.

**Headline metric.** Report the fraction of the strong-proposer gain retained by the self arm: (self − base) / (Opus − base). ACE's Table 16 is the template (+7.6 for same-model vs +7.8 for the stronger reflector). This turns the objection into a result.

**How to phrase the claim:**
- If the self arm gains substantially: *"a frozen Qwen3.8-27B evolves its own harness from execution feedback; a stronger proposer adds X points (knowledge-transfer premium)."*
- If only the Opus arm wins: *"teacher-guided / external-proposer harness evolution"*, the same category as Meta-Harness and ADAS.
- In either case, avoid "RSI". With a fixed AppWorld evaluator and a non-self-modifying proposer, Chen et al.'s term is *bounded self-refinement*. If you want a recursion claim, the proposer has to edit its own prompts or procedures (STOP/DGM-style), and you must disclose that the evaluator is fixed.
- On AppWorld, expect reviewers to know ACE and Self-Harness. Both deliberately use the same model in all roles.

## 6. Open questions for you

1. **Can Qwen3.8-27B reliably write harness patches as a proposer?** STOP and Live-SWE-agent show weak self-proposers can make things *worse*. A small pilot of the self arm should come first, because it decides whether the paper's headline is "self-evolving" or "teacher-guided".
2. **What exactly does the diagnoser see?** Raw AppWorld test outputs, ground-truth state diffs, or only pass/fail? Anything beyond what the executor sees at test time is a second confound, separate from proposer strength.
3. **Do you want a recursion claim at all?** Letting the proposer evolve its own diagnosis and proposal prompts is cheap to add and changes the framing. It also adds a component to ablate before the January deadline.
